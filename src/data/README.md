# Data

Your product's data seam. A collection is read through `read()`: the pages, actions and the assistant that show records (members, keys, requests, customers, tickets, tests, releases) go through it. `collections.ts` lists your collections: one description of each set of records, with what may be created, changed and removed, read by the pages, changed by their server actions and offered to the assistant as tools (`docs/assistant.md`). Put other types and queries beside it (`src/data/<product>.ts`), and import them from your pages and views.

- Until your API is wired, return deterministic sample data: a seeded generator and a fixed clock, never `Math.random()` or `new Date()` in render, or the server and the client disagree and React reports a hydration error.
- Give anything a tile or a chart shows over time one value per day: the Meridian cursor reads it.
- Read freshness from when the data arrived, never from now.
- Return `null` for "not measured"; the formatters in `src/lib/format.ts` render it as a dash.
- Shape records to the types the patterns define (`ActivityEvent` in the activity feed, `Service` in the status list), so they render without adapters. Where a record holds more than a card draws, shape it in the read: an incident's record names its ticket by id and the Incident card takes a title and an address, so `readIncidents()` in `metrics.ts` reads the tickets the incidents name, by their ids, and hands a page the card's `Incident` with the ticket's title and the address the Tickets page finds it at (`/operations-tickets?q=<its title>`). A ticket that cannot be read, because the read failed or the person may not read tickets, or that is not found leaves the incident standing without one.

Two more files carry the access seam. `access.ts` says who the current request is and what they may do: `resolveAccess()` returns a scope (tenant, subject and an authorization key), `collectionFor(scope, name)` returns that scope's own collection, and `can(scope, name, op, ids?)` answers whether an operation, and every record it touches, is allowed. `read.ts` is how a page reads: `read(name, query)` resolves access first, refuses what the caller may not read, and caches the result under a tag for that tenant's collection. A server action authorizes with `can`, writes through `collectionFor`, and after the commit calls `updateTag(collectionTag(tenantId, name))`, so the next read is fresh.

The sample's policy is five personas on one tenant (`PERSONAS` in `src/data/access.ts`), and its collections live in one process's memory, so a second server process would not see a write. A product replaces the policy with its session and binds each collection's database predicate to the scope: a cache key alone is not a permission check, and a read it cannot key on what the caller may see must stay uncached.

A list a person pages through is read on the server: `requests.ts` turns the address into one validated `Query` (filters as conditions, the page as an offset), reads one page with its total through `read()`, and works the summary its tiles draw out of the same filtered set, so a page sends 20 rows and never the whole set. A product computes that summary in its database (a count, a share, a percentile and a time bucket are one aggregate query); the sample reads the newest 500 matching rows and says so when the set is larger. Whatever the database is, the collection's key must be the final sort tiebreaker, as `normalizeQuery` makes it here, or a row can repeat or vanish across pages. The CSV export (`app/api/export/requests/route.ts`) authorizes itself, queries `collectionFor(scope, name)` in batches of at most 100 uncached, and stops when the request is aborted; `src/lib/csv.ts` encodes each cell and guards a text value a spreadsheet would read as a formula.

Most of what a page draws is a collection, read through `read()` or with `collectionFor` and asked under its own grant, so a second tenant sees its own rows. Usage' weekday-and-hour heatmap and its regions are the `hours` and `regions` collections: when traffic comes and where it comes from is a job of the Business home, and a figure that is not a row is read by no scope and checked by no grant. `usageFigures()` in `usage.ts` asks `hours:read` and `regions:read`, refuses with `AccessDenied` when either is missing, and takes every row of each from the scope's own collection, unsorted and unlimited (`read()` sorts by the key and answers a hundred rows, which is neither what the regions' order nor 168 hours want). The hours of the day are a sum over the grid, not a third collection.

The sample is one company that sells tokens, priced once, and its records agree with each other because of how each is made (`src/system/fixtures/company.ts`). Six collections hold it:

| Collection | A row is | Read by |
|---|---|---|
| `days` | A day (UTC): `requests`, `errors` (5xx and 429), `p95` in ms, `tokens`, `revenue` in US dollars and `revenueByPlan` (`Enterprise`, `Scale`, `Starter`) | Every role |
| `models` | What is sold, four rows (`meridian-large`, `meridian-swift`, `meridian-embed`, and `none` for the calls that reach no model): the route it `serves`, `inputPrice` and `outputPrice` in US dollars a million tokens, `callPrice` in US dollars a call, each null where it has none | Every role |
| `usage` | A day and a model, keyed `<date>:<model>`: `requests`, `inputTokens`, `outputTokens`, `revenue`, `errors`, `p95` in ms, `ttft` (p95 ms to the first token) and `tps` (output tokens a second at the median), the last two null for a model that writes no tokens | Every role |
| `compute` | A day and a region, keyed `<date>:<region>`: `gpus` provisioned and `used`, the GPU-hours used of the `gpus` x 24 offered | Every role |
| `costs` | A day: `compute`, what the GPUs provisioned cost in US dollars | An Owner and an Admin only (`costs:read`) |
| `customers` | A customer: its `plan`, its `status` (`active`, `trial`, `past due` or `left`), its `requests`, `tokens`, `revenue` and `byEndpoint` (requests by route) over the last 30 complete days, its 14-day `trend`, `errorRate`, `seats`, `region`, its `owner` (a member's name), and the days it `joined`, changed plan (`planSince`, with `previousPlan`), `left`, its trial ends (`trialEnds`) and since when it is past due (`pastDueSince`), each `YYYY-MM-DD` or null | Every role |

A model's day is its tokens at the model's price, to the cent, and a day is the sum of its four `usage` rows; a day's revenue is divided among the plans and a plan's 30 days among its customers. So over the last 30 complete days revenue is the same to the cent by day, by model, by plan and by customer, and requests are the same by day, by model, by customer, by endpoint, by region, by status class and by weekday and hour. `endpoints`, `regions`, `responses` and `hours` are snapshots of those 30 days: they do not follow the period a page is set to. A customer that has left keeps its record and is counted in no figure of customers, revenue or requests: `kept()` and `customerFigures()` in `src/views/customers-context.ts` are the one place that is decided, and the Customers list shows such a customer under its own status only. A read answers 100 rows unless it asks for more and never more than 500, so a count or a sum over customers starts from `readAll()`, never from a first page. A product replaces each `rows` with its own query, and computes the snapshots in its database.

Three collections hold the operation. `services` is each monitored service: its state now, its latency now and its last 90 days, oldest first, the last of them the day in progress, which is its state now. Its `uptime` is worked out from those days by one rule, `uptimeOf()` in `src/system/fixtures/sample.ts`: a degraded day costs half an hour of the day's 1,440 minutes and a day of outage two hours, so an uptime is 1 exactly when no day carries a mark, and no strip of days reads 90% or less. Every complete day the company was outside its band is a day at least one service marks. `incidents` holds each incident with its updates, its `owner` (a member's name) and its `ticketId` (the ticket its work is tracked in), each null when there is none; that ticket names the incident back as its `incidentId`. One incident is open, with three updates, an owner and a ticket, and three are resolved. `requests` is a sample of the requests received, never all of them: a count of requests comes from `days` and `usage`. A request holds `ttft`, the milliseconds from its arrival to its first token, never more than its `latency`, and null where no model answered because the request reached none or was refused. A product works an uptime out of what it measures and keeps the agreement: a strip with a mark never reads 100%.

The four daily collections, `days`, `usage`, `compute` and `costs`, hold the 360 complete days the product keeps and, as their last rows, the day in progress: the clock's own day (2026-10-03 in the sample) with what has arrived by the clock, 09:00 UTC. Its requests, tokens, revenue and errors are part of a day's, a region's `used` is of the nine hours it has had, and its cost is its GPUs for nine hours; it is made as every day is, so its four `usage` rows still sum to its `days` row and its revenue by plan to its revenue. Each description says the last row may be a day still in progress, so an agent that sums a collection leaves it out. No period, total, comparison, customer's figure or snapshot counts it.

A period is complete days, counted back from yesterday. `readDays(period)` in `metrics.ts` is the one read of them, and returns five things: `series`, the period's complete days, oldest first, the latest 7, 30 or 90 of them; `previous`, the same number of complete days before those, fewer where fewer are kept; `totals`, which holds `current` and `previous`, each the requests, error rate, median p95 and revenue of its days, with `previous` null when there are no days before; `running`, the day in progress on its own, or null when the latest row kept is a complete day; and `observedAt`. For `all`, `series` is every complete day kept, `previous` is empty and `totals.previous` is null: there is nothing to compare all of history with, so a tile draws no change and a context says no previous period is kept. No constant stands for `all`. `readDays` reads every row with `readAll()`, which pages past the 500 rows one read returns (`usage` and `compute` hold 1,444), and a product asks its database for the window instead. A figure, its change, a median, a busiest day and every finding are worked out from `series` and `previous`, never from `running`: that day is handed over for a chart of days to draw as running, and Home's context says it is still in progress and counted in no total.

What is still a constant is imported from `src/data/sample.ts`, with no `read()`, no access check and no tenant: the clock (`DEMO_NOW`, `DEMO_UPDATED_AT`), the region names the request filter offers (`REGION_NAMES`), the member statuses and teams (`STATUSES`, `TEAMS`), the pure functions a request's page works its trace, payloads and cost out with, and the types. `usageOf()` prices a request from the `models` records and from no list of its own: its tokens at its model's prices a million, in and out, the price of a call when it reached no model, and nothing when it was refused; the request's page and its context print what it returns. They are one fixed set for every caller, and a client view imports them: so `sample.ts` takes nothing from the module that generates the company's records, which only the server reads (`tests/client-imports.test.ts` walks the client modules' imports and fails if one reaches it). The 30 days the sign-in page draws before anyone is signed in come from `signInDays()` in `sign-in.ts`, a day's date and its requests and nothing else. A product replaces each import with its own read, and serves anything per tenant through a collection, as customers, tickets, verification runs and releases already are, or a second tenant would see the first's data.

Three collections hold the engineering work. `tickets` is the team's work against a service; `tests` holds the verification runs (a suite run against one endpoint of a release, with its verdict) and `releases` the versions on their way to an environment, each with the pairs of suite and endpoint it requires. A release is `testing` exactly while some required pair has no run, or its latest run failed or was skipped; the latest run is the one that started last. Only `tickets` is written: `ticketsFor(scope)` in `ticket-mutations.ts` is the one boundary every create and change passes, bound by `access.ts` so a page action and an agent's tool apply the same rules. It sets a ticket's id, its state (a new ticket is open) and its times from `clock()`, checks the service and the assignee against the collections themselves, allows only the five steps between open, waiting and closed, and changes all the named tickets or none. A change that leaves a ticket as it is does not stamp it. `closedAt` follows the state, ticket by ticket: a ticket a change moves into closed gets the clock's time, one it moves back to open gets null, and in a change to several a ticket already closed keeps the time it has. A new ticket has no closing time and tracks no incident. Neither `closedAt` nor `incidentId` is a caller's to set: a change that names one is refused as a change to the id or a time is, and both are `pageOnlyFields`, so an agent reads them and no agent tool asks for one.

`verification.ts` holds the three rules that turn runs and releases into what a page reads: `latestRuns` (one run for each release, suite and endpoint, the one that started last, ties broken by the id as plain text), `verificationOf` (how many of a release's required verifications passed and which are missing, failed or skipped) and `currentVersions` (the release that went out last for each service and environment). It imports nothing from the server, so a client view and the assistant's context use the same functions. `engineering.ts` holds the two reads, `readRuns` (every run, with its release's version, service, environment and commit) and `readReleases` (every release, with its verification); the Tests and Releases pages and their view tools read there, and neither page nor tool writes anything.

Three reads look across the product: Home's read and Now's across all of it, and the bell's. Home's read (`homeTool` in `src/views/tools.ts`) reads `days`, `usage`, `models`, `compute`, `customers`, `services`, `incidents`, `releases` and `tickets`, each apart from the others, so one that cannot be read leaves its part missing and the rest standing; it reads `costs` only when `can(scope, 'costs', 'read')` says the person may, the question `read()` asks, so a Member or a Viewer causes no read of it and is handed no figure in its place. A year's windows are each worked out by `totalsOf` in `metrics.ts`, the function a period's totals are. Now's read (`nowTool`) reads `services`, `incidents` with their tickets, `releases` and `tests` (through `readReleases` and `readRuns`), `days`, `usage`, `models`, `compute`, `endpoints`, `tickets` (through `readTickets`, so each names its assignee) and `activity`, each apart; `readActivity()` in `metrics.ts` is the newest changes, newest first, through `read()`. Who is reading is `resolveAccess().subjectId`, read on the server, so a ticket is marked as the reader's own only in the reader's own read. `main.ts` holds `attempt`, which makes a read that throws a part that is missing, not zero: Home's read, Now's and the bell read each collection through it, so one that cannot be read leaves the rest standing. `alerts.ts` holds `readAlerts`, what the shell's bell shows: every unresolved incident, one alert for the customers past due (the customer's name when there is one, how many when there are more), how many verifications are failing and every high-priority ticket nobody has, each worked out from a read the caller may make and linked to the page that handles it. Neither is typed anywhere; a product writes its own exceptions the same way.

The template's own sample, ZZ Meridian's own dashboard, lives in `src/system/fixtures/`, because the Design Atlas and every card preview read it; `collections.ts` serves it as each collection's `rows`. `sample.ts` there holds the clock, the names, the shapes and the request log, and `company.ts` the company's generated records. Leave it in place, and replace each `rows` with your API and `clock` with `new Date()`.
