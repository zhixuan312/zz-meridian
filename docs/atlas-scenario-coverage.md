# Atlas scenario coverage

This is the inventory of the whole Meridian sample: every job the product is meant to serve, every page, the shell, and every pattern and chart, each with what becomes of it and why. It is a review table, written before the three-workspace product is built and updated as it is: the work is planned from it in phases, and a review of every disposition closes it. No runtime code reads it, and `tests/cli-atlas-inventory.test.ts` is the only thing that parses it.

## How to read it

- One table, one row per job, page, shell, pattern or chart. The columns are the twelve inventory fields.
- `scenarioId` is `S1` to `S12` (the mandatory jobs), `A1` to `A8` (the adjacent candidates, numbered in the order the spec lists them: bulk actions, saved views, board-style work, comparison tables, advanced charts, approval queues, notification centres, onboarding checklists) or `shared` for a widget no single scenario owns.
- The layer column is the Atlas layer: L1 shell, L3 pattern or chart, L4 page or page template, L5 workspace level.
- `existingSource` names the folders the row is about. A page is named by the folder that holds its spec. A scenario row names the closest existing composition. The view under `src/views/` that a page row renders is named in `currentPageOrWidget`.
- `disposition` is `keep`, `compose`, `replace` or `remove`; `defer` appears only on an A row. A `keep` row means the thing does its job as the source stands. A `compose` row means the job is met by arranging existing parts, possibly through a new template. A `replace` row means the current thing does not meet the job.
- `deliveredSource`, `atlasEntry` and `verification` hold a path or route once something exists. On a `keep` row the delivered source is the folder itself and the Atlas entry is its existing route. `planned: Phase N` means the outline phase in which the work is planned: Phase 1 foundations and compositions, Phase 2 product tasks and data, Phase 3 navigation and shell, Phase 4 Atlas coverage and disposition review.
- `applicableStates` lists only the states the page spec or source describes: normal, loading, empty, filtered-empty, error, partial, read-only. A state that is not listed was not found in the source.
- `verification` is `planned: Phase 4` for every row that no executable check covers yet. A preview existing is not counted as verification.
- Nothing here says how a page looks. No render was looked at while writing it, and source inspection is not visual verification. The Phase 4 review and the Phase 5 acceptance cover appearance.
- No cell contains a pipe character.

## What this inventory already says

- Customers and Requests repeat one arrangement (summary tiles over a filter bar and a Data table). The records template exists for it; Customers is its first consumer and Requests its second, and both are built from it.
- S6, S7 and S8 have their pages and data. S6 also has its mutation; S7 and S8 have none, and the sample records state and shows verdicts, it does not run tests or deploy.
- S11 is the only `replace` in the table: navigation was a flat list with no workspace concept. The rail and the shell have an optional navigation input; the declaration of three workspaces is built and the route adapter that supplies it is Phase 3's.
- S12 shares its loading composition (the page-loading card); empty, filtered-empty and error stay with the Data table and the Empty state, and each page's README describes its own.
- Single-consumer cards: Prose (only inside the assistant), Appearance menu (only inside the rail), Form section (Settings), Detail head (request detail), Composition bar (Overview), Heatmap (Analytics). No card has zero consumers; every pattern and chart is used by a page, a shell part or another card.
- Settings builds its own Appearance section for preferences the rail's Appearance menu also sets. Two surfaces exist for one job.
- Period select is used by two pages and Freshness by four of nine; the others have no stated period or freshness. Whether that is deliberate belongs to the Phase 2 recomposition.
- Members and API keys share a table-plus-sheet arrangement with access gating; their bodies are built from the records template, and their behaviour is kept.
- The four embed views reuse the console's cards, so a change to a shared card reaches them; they have no row-level work of their own.
- Of the eight adjacent candidates, seven are deferred (A1 to A6 and A8) and one is composed (A7, the alerts panel plus the Main exceptions view). None is built as a new feature.
- There is no customer detail page and no list of proposals.
