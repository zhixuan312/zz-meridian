# Atlas scenario coverage

This is the inventory of the whole Meridian sample: every job the product is meant to serve, every page, the shell, and every pattern and chart, each with what becomes of it and why. It is a review table. It was written before the three-workspace product was built and kept up to date as it was built, and it now records the review of every disposition. No runtime code reads it. `tests/cli-atlas-inventory.test.ts` holds its shape, and `tests/cli-atlas-review.test.ts` holds that nothing in it is left open: every Atlas address it names is served and every check it names exists.

## How to read it

- One table, one row per job, page, shell, pattern or chart, and one row for each thing the work took out of the product. The columns are the twelve inventory fields.
- `scenarioId` is `S1` to `S12` (the mandatory jobs), `A1` to `A8` (the adjacent candidates, numbered in the order the spec lists them: bulk actions, saved views, board-style work, comparison tables, advanced charts, approval queues, notification centres, onboarding checklists) or `shared` for a widget no single scenario owns. A row that records a removal carries the id of the scenario it belonged to, or `shared`.
- The layer column is the Atlas layer: L1 shell, L3 pattern or chart, L4 page or page template, L5 workspace level.
- `existingSource` names the folders the row is about. For a removal it names where the thing was. A page is named by the folder that holds its spec. A scenario row names the closest existing composition. The view under `src/views/` that a page row renders is named in `currentPageOrWidget`.
- `disposition` is `keep`, `compose`, `replace` or `remove`; `defer` appears only on an A row. A `keep` row means the thing does its job as the source stands. A `compose` row means the job is met by arranging existing parts, possibly through a template. A `replace` row means the current thing did not meet the job and was replaced. A `remove` row means the work took the thing out because nothing stood behind it, and its delivered source is what does the job now, or the page that remains.
- No cell is waiting for a later piece of work. Every cell says what is there or says `none` and why. A question left for the stakeholder at the end of the findings is about new work the product might grow, never about a cell: each cell records what was decided and built. A deferred candidate has `none` for its delivered source, states and verification, and its reason says that nothing was built and what would bring it back.
- `deliveredSource` holds the paths that make the row true. `atlasEntry` holds the Atlas addresses, in backticks, where the thing is specified, or `none: ` followed by the reason there is no entry. Every address in the column is served by the Atlas, as `node scripts/vitals.ts --atlas --list` prints it.
- `applicableStates` lists only the states the page spec or source describes: normal, loading, empty, filtered-empty, error, partial, read-only. A state that is not listed was not found in the source.
- `verification` names, in backticks, the test file, the journey or the script that exercises the row. A preview existing is not counted as verification. A row that names `scripts/audit.ts` alone is checked by the Atlas's own browser audit of its entry (every width, both themes) and by no test of its own: nothing else exercises it. A row that also names a test is exercised by that test as well.
- Nothing here says how a page looks. No render was looked at while writing it, and source inspection is not visual verification. The independent review that closes the work looks at appearance.
- No cell contains a pipe character.

## What the review found

- **The records arrangement is one template.** Seven pages are tiles over a filterable table: Customers, Requests, API keys, Members, Tickets, Tests and Releases. They are all built from the Records template in `src/components/templates/records`, which also owns the loading arrangement, so the arrangement is written once. API keys and Members use it without summary tiles.
- **Page loading is one card.** Every dashboard route's loading state and the page fallbacks import the Page loading card, `src/components/patterns/page-loading`, which is the old shared helper file moved; each route still composes its own shapes.
- **The customer figures are one calculation.** `customerFigures` in `src/views/customers-context.ts` is behind Customers' tiles, its context, Home's lines worth knowing, Revenue and the bell's customers past due, so the pages cannot disagree about a count.
- **The two write boundaries share their machinery.** Members and tickets are written through `src/data/member-mutations.ts` and `src/data/ticket-mutations.ts`; the write queue and the invalidation after a write live once in `src/data/write-boundary.ts`.
- **Home and the bell use the detail pages' own functions.** Home's Business and Engineering cards and its lines worth knowing are worked out by `homeData()` in `src/views/home-parts.ts` from every record, using the functions the pages for customers, tickets, verification runs and releases use for their own tiles. `readAlerts()` reads the same sources, so a figure on Home, a tile on its page and an alert agree.
- **The flat navigation was replaced.** The navigation was a single list. It is now a declaration of three workspaces in `src/app.config.ts`, with each entry naming its workspace, derived per workspace by `src/views/workspaces.ts` and supplied to the chrome by the product's adapter, `src/views/workspace-chrome.tsx`. Access did not move: a workspace is where a person looks, and each page's feature decides what they may do.
- **The typed alerts, the typed count on the rail and Settings' connected hosts were removed.** The alerts are now worked out from incidents, customers, verification runs and tickets by `readAlerts()`. The rail's Services entry has no count, and the bell lists what needs a person. The connected hosts, which came from a fixture, are gone; Settings keeps the true statement that every proposed change waits for approval.
- **Single-consumer cards are kept, each for a reason.** The Heatmap answers when traffic comes. Detail head is the request detail's masthead, and the request is the one record whose page draws it. Form section is the one form page's building block, and it reports unsaved changes to the shell's guard. Prose renders the assistant's messages safely, and Assistant is the shell's panel. Copy field serves the one-time secret on API keys and Switch serves the Assistant section in Settings.
- **Breadcrumb, Progress, Radio group and Tabs are components of the system that the sample does not draw.** They are used by no page, and nothing but the registry imports them. They are kept because removing a card breaks every project that uses it, and the review accounts for the sample's widgets without shrinking the catalogue to the sample.
- **Icons, Motion and Planes are specifications of layer 1.** They are base cards with no component behind them, and they say how the system draws, moves and layers things.
- **The heatmap, the hours of the day and the regions are kept, and are now rows.** They answer when traffic comes and where it comes from, which is Usage's job, in Business (S2). They had been constants behind a check of another collection's grant; they are now the rows of two read-only collections, `hours` (requests for each weekday and hour) and `regions` (requests and median latency for each region), served to the tenant and asked under their own grants, which every main role holds. The hours of the day are a sum over the first collection. Two reads for three figures is the minimal model: the third figure is derived from the first, and a collection for each shape would store one fact twice. The rows are the sample's own fixed shapes, not worked out from the request log or the daily totals, so they do not move when those do.
- **Delete workspace was removed, with its action and its grant.** The control deleted nothing and reported that it had. With it went the `delete-workspace` action, the Owner's `workspace:remove` grant, the collection's remove operation and the page's flag, because a right nothing can exercise is dead. A product that wants a real one decides first what deleting its workspace does to its keys, its logs and every session, whether it can be undone, and who may do it; then it adds the control, the action and the grant together.
- **S3 has a customer page.** The job is to find a customer by plan and status and to understand usage and spend; the list holds all of it, and a row opens that customer's own page at `/customers/<id>`: where it stands and since when, who has it on our side, its week, what it paid with its share of revenue, its error rate beside the product's, what it asks for and its latest requests. Every customer named in the product opens it: the request log's cells, a request's page, Revenue, Home and the bell. The Detail head card's preview still draws a customer's head as a specimen of the card.
- **Usage states requests over the period, and Revenue the spend.** S2 asks for usage and spend changes over a period: Usage's title and line state requests, the error rate and the median day's p95 against the period before, from the totals the Home reads and under its labels, and Revenue, Business's home, states what customers paid. The hours, the regions, the endpoints and the responses are fixed figures of the last 30 complete days: each card and the context name that window, and neither claims the period for them.
- **The sign-in page lost a form that sent nothing.** The rendered review of the whole product found the page's second panel reporting an emailed sign-in link and an opened company sign-in with no action behind either, a promise of a free workspace, and the working panel titled as a demo. The form is removed and the working panel says Sign in as.
- **Three controls were removed for reporting what did not happen, and one confirmation was reworded.** Customers' Invite customer validated a form, said "Invitation sent" and wrote nothing. Services' Subscribe to updates said updates would go to an address and kept nothing. Settings' time zone was saved to the workspace record and read by nothing: every date is cut on the configuration's `app.timezone`. Each went, with the fixture or the field behind it. The request's menu item that said Revoke the key only opened API keys, and is now called Open API keys. Inviting a member writes a record with the status Invited and delivers nothing, so the confirmation says the member is listed as Invited, and no longer that an invitation was sent or that they join when they accept.
- **The workspace's name has one reader.** Settings saved a name that nothing else read, and said the rail showed it. An invitation to join now names the workspace by its record, read for the people who may invite, and Settings and the guide say only that. The rail shows the product's name from the configuration.
- **A count opens the records it counts.** Tests' Failing now and the bell's failing verifications count the latest run of each verification, and their link opened every failed run, the replaced ones too; the Tests page gained a Runs filter (all, or the latest of each) and the links use it. The bell's alert for a ticket nobody owns opened every high-priority ticket; it opens the list on that ticket. A ticket whose owner has left the workspace is unowned on the page, for the assistant and in the bell alike, by one function. Services lists every incident nobody has resolved, not only the newest, so each incident Main and the bell name is on the page they open.
- **Customers' tiles draw no trend.** Two tiles carried a sparkline worked out from the customers' request trends by a constant, which was no series of customers or of revenue. A tile draws a trend only where a series stands behind it.
- **Settings has two surfaces for appearance, by design.** The Appearance section and the rail's menu write the same preferences, so a change in one shows in the other. The rail's menu is at hand on every page, and Settings is where a person looks for it.
- **Settings holds what a record or the device stands behind.** Workspace is a record, Appearance and the Assistant switch are stored on the device, and Agents and MCP states the one rule that holds. There is no Notifications section: the sample sends nothing, and a switch with no sender behind it promised something it could not do. The Rail preview's hand-drawn item states went for a like reason; the real Rail is drawn beside them.
- **No second template was made.** The analytical pages (Home, Revenue, Usage, Now, Services) each arrange different patterns, and the two detail pages, a request and a customer, arrange different parts. A template for one page would be a wrapper made to fill a layer. Records stays the one template.
- **Questions left for the stakeholder.** Each is about new work; none holds a cell open. Should the sample grow notification preferences, with a record that holds them and a sender behind them? That would be new work, and the section would come back with it. Should the sample have a real Delete workspace, after the decisions above? Should the rail show the workspace's name from its record, and should a workspace keep a time zone that every date is then cut on? Should a customer be invited from the console, with a record written and a message sent?
